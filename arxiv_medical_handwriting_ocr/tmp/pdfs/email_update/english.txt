Comparative Benchmarking of Neural OCR Methods for
Handwritten Word Recognition in Medical Prescription Images
under Limited-Data and Computational Constraints
Samuel F. Santos∗
Universidade Estadual de Santa Cruz

Abstract
This study presents a practical comparison of CNN-based and Transformer-based models
for handwritten word recognition in medical prescription images. Two source datasets,
RxHandBD and Doctor’s Handwritten PrescriptionBD, were evaluated individually and in a
combined training scenario. From-scratch training was used for a CNN baseline formulated
as a closed-set word classifier and for a CNN-BiLSTM sequence-recognition model, whereas
the pre-trained TrOCR and PARSeq Transformers were fine-tuned for character-sequence
transcription. The study examines how dataset characteristics, training regimes, and task
formulations affect recognition performance, model limitations, and training cost under
limited-data conditions.

Keywords: Neural Networks; Convolution; Transformers; Artificial Intelligence.

Introduction

During medical appointments, patients are often issued a medical prescription containing information about medications and treatment instructions intended to support their recovery.
However, the readability of these prescriptions is frequently affected by the physician’s handwriting and the context in which they are written. Factors such as the urgency of medical care and
the high demand for patient consultations can negatively impact the legibility of handwritten
prescriptions, making their interpretation more challenging for both healthcare professionals and
patients.
As a consequence, medical prescriptions often present low legibility, making their interpretation challenging for both pharmacists and patients. This difficulty has led several countries to
adopt digital prescription systems as a way to reduce interpretation errors and improve patient
safety. However, this transition has not occurred universally, and handwritten prescriptions are
still widely used in many healthcare settings.
Therefore, it is important to investigate and understand the challenges of applying Optical
Character Recognition (OCR) to handwritten medical prescriptions, including both textual
characters and medical symbols. Furthermore, it is relevant to evaluate whether the knowledge
and techniques developed for this domain can be generalized to other types of handwritten
documents.
